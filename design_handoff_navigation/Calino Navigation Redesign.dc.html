Turn 4 — the expanded "···" pill, redesigned

Three takes on the widened panel

Cropped to just the pill so it's easy to compare. Same content everywhere (Search, Settings, 8 views) — different visual treatments.

4a Icon grid, no beige track
  • Every view gets a real icon, not just text — faster to scan, feels more "app-like".
  • No beige track anywhere; cards float on the cream background with a thin hairline border instead.
  • Trade-off: 8 custom icons is more visual noise, and some (Week vs Agenda) are hard to tell apart at a glance.
4b Minimal list, hairline dividers
  • Reads like the rest of the app's editorial lists (Tasks/Journal) instead of an app-store-style icon grid.
  • Active view marked with a small accent dot rather than a filled pill — quieter.
  • Most compact vertically of the three — good if the pill's height is the bigger concern.
4c Compact toolbar strip
tap for search or settings
  • Search/Settings shrink to icon-only buttons — no card treatment, just quiet toolbar buttons like the hamburger.
  • One consistent beige-track language for both rows (feels like a single control, not two different components).
  • Closest in spirit to the very first "widen the switcher" idea — smallest visual delta from what exists today.
Turn 3 — converging

Best of both

Fair points on both: edge-swipe is unreliable in a browser/PWA (fights back-gesture and address-bar reveal), and icon-only segments need learning. 3a keeps 2a's clean layout and swipe-feedback dots, but puts the hamburger back as a visible, tappable icon and keeps view labels as text.
